Using large language models to help recognize a rare pediatric lung disease earlier

About the Project

Primary ciliary dyskinesia (PCD) is a rare, inherited disease in which the tiny hair-like structures (cilia) that clear mucus from the airways do not work properly. Its early signs, such as a chronic wet cough and repeated ear, sinus, and chest infections, look a lot like common childhood illnesses, so many children wait years for a correct diagnosis.

We study whether large language models (LLMs) can help clinicians recognize PCD sooner by reading routine clinical notes. In our first study, open-source LLMs reviewed de-identified notes from children's first pulmonology visits and flagged which children might have PCD, with clinicians assessing both the answers and the reasoning behind them. The models caught nearly all true cases but also flagged many children without PCD, which suggests they are best suited as an early-warning aid that supports, rather than replaces, specialist judgment.

This is an ongoing research program. We are currently working on further projects at the intersection of AI and rare disease. Check back for updates.
